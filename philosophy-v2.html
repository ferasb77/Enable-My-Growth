02 THE VANTAGE POINT PERSPECTIVE · CONTEXT · INTERPRETATION

Every consequential decision is made from a particular vantage point.

That perspective shapes which facts appear relevant, which explanations feel plausible, which risks receive attention, and which possibilities remain unseen.

“Perspective changes what becomes possible.”
03 THE DISCIPLINE OF DISTINCTION

What is observed, inferred, unknown, and disputed should remain distinguishable.

The aim is not to remove intuition or replace professional experience with a rigid process. It is to give judgment enough structure to distinguish observation from interpretation, evidence from confidence, and conviction from pressure.

04 FIVE COMMITMENTS

How the philosophy behaves in practice.

01

Professional judgment first

Structure should enhance the judgment of responsible people, not replace their accountability.

02

Context before interpretation

A signal acquires meaning through the conditions, constraints, relationships, and incentives around it.

03

Evidence before confidence

Confidence is not evidence. What is observed, inferred, unknown, and disputed should remain distinguishable.

04

Conditions before conviction

Examine what made a conclusion convincing before relying on it as an account of what is happening.

05

Learning after action

A decision is not finished when it is made. Outcomes should refine the reasoning that produced them.

05 BOUNDARIES

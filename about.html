Feras Banna works where leadership, human behavior, and consequential decisions meet.

Professional practice

For more than twenty-five years, I have supported leaders, teams, and organizations across seven countries—in government, banking, energy, healthcare, telecommunications, infrastructure, and the private sector. The settings have varied—executive assessment, leadership development, organizational effectiveness, facilitated dialogue, and advisory work—but one question keeps returning:

What conditions are shaping the judgment we are seeing here?

That question became the foundation of Enable My Growth: a founder-led practice for examining context, evidence, assumptions, and conditions before reaching consequential conclusions.

Author & intellectual work

Across more than twenty-five years of work with people and organizations, I have repeatedly worked close to the point where information becomes interpretation, interpretation becomes judgment, and judgment begins changing what happens next.

That question eventually became Conditions Before Conviction, currently in development, about how conclusions become convincing enough for us to rely on, and what our certainty changes once we act.

Professional experience gives me proximity to the question. It does not make the answer true. I am not interested in explaining why other people think badly. I am interested in the moment when any of us begins treating an explanation as settled enough to rely on.

Part of the question grew somewhere else entirely. An earlier technical venture, Banna-Venturi, and a validation attempt that failed, made the question of what evidence permits us to claim personal rather than academic. That question later contributed to Phys-Valid, a technical validation and governance system. Looking back, I can see a developmental line from that engineering question to the human one this book asks.

Perspective
Most explanations arrive too quickly.

Organizations explain performance through personality. Leaders explain hesitation through motivation. Teams explain disagreement through resistance. These explanations may contain truth, yet they can also close inquiry before the conditions have been examined.

My role is to create enough structure and space for a better question to emerge—and then help the people responsible make a decision they can genuinely own.

How I work

The form follows the context. I may work as an advisor, assessor, facilitator, development partner, or speaker—and often combine those modes deliberately. What remains constant is a respect for professional judgment, disciplined inquiry, and the human responsibility at the center of every consequential choice.
